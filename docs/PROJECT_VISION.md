## 1. The concept

A scroll-directed science-fiction short film in which an astronaut (you) leaves home, launches from Earth, crosses the solar system, passes the edge of human exploration and reaches an unbuilt universe. The physical journey **is** the professional journey:

| Journey | Professional meaning |
|---|---|
| Home | Where you come from and why you build |
| Ship and launch | Committing to engineering as a career |
| Solar system | What you know, where you've worked and what you've built |
| The edge of the known | What you are learning now |
| The unknown universe | What you want to build next |
| Final transmission | How to reach you |

The visitor doesn't browse sections. They **watch a directed journey**, scroll to control time, and stop to interact only where it's worth it.

### The one-sentence pitch
*"A developer's career told as a single continuous mission, from a bedroom desk to a universe that doesn't exist yet."*

---

## 2. The governing metaphor (most important rule) [RELAXED B3: content now mostly appears on ship interfaces; projects remain human-made structures]

Every visual decision should follow one consistent rule, so the story never feels arbitrary:

> **Natural bodies represent who you are and what you know.**
> **Human-made structures represent what you have built and done.**

- Planets, the Moon, the Sun and stars hold **identity, education and skills**.
- Stations, outposts, beacons and probes hold **projects, experience and achievements**.

This rule means projects never sit "on a planet for no reason". A project is always an **engineered object in orbit**, which is both scientifically plausible and true to the metaphor: you built it.

## 3. Creative direction

### Tone
Grounded, quiet, awe-driven science fiction. Closer to *Apollo 11* (2019 documentary), *Interstellar*, *The Martian*, *For All Mankind* and *2001* than to arcade sci-fi. Confident restraint: few elements, each one deliberate.

### Visual pillars
1. **One light source tells the story.** In space the Sun is the only key light. Its direction, intensity and colour temperature change with distance, so the visitor feels where they are without being told.
2. **Real materials, real scale cues.** Brushed metal, worn fabric, ceramic heat tiles and dusty regolith. Scale comes from things passing close to the camera (debris, ring particles, ice crystals).
3. **Instrument-grade interface.** Text looks like it belongs to the ship: monochrome, thin rules, small caps, coordinate readouts and one accent colour. Never glowing cards.
4. **Darkness is a design element.** Negative space and black frame the content. Most frames should be about 70% dark.
5. **Each destination has a signature colour and light condition**, so no two planet scenes look alike (see section 9).

### Things we deliberately avoid
Neon cyberpunk palettes, glassmorphism cards floating in space, logos orbiting planets, particle explosions without purpose, game HUDs (health bars, reticles, minimaps), lens flares on everything, and "AI template" gradients.

## 4. Visitor experience

### Three visitor types, three paths
| Visitor | Need | Path |
|---|---|---|
| **Explorer** (curious dev, creative peer) | The full experience | Scroll the whole journey (target: 3–4 minutes) |
| **Recruiter / hiring manager** | Facts in under 60 seconds | Persistent **Resume** and **Mission Brief** buttons, plus chapter jump |
| **Constrained visitor** (mobile, low-end GPU, reduced motion, screen reader) | The same content, no friction | **Mission Brief mode**: a fully designed, non-WebGL, accessible version of every word in the experience |

**Mission Brief mode is non-negotiable.** It protects you from the main risk of cinematic portfolios: a recruiter who never finds your experience. It also covers SEO, accessibility and crawlers.

### Emotional arc
Intimacy (home) → anticipation (cockpit) → exhilaration (launch) → wonder (planets) → awe and humility (Sun, deep space) → mystery (wormhole) → resolution and invitation (transmission).

## 6. Environment descriptions

### A. The house: "Origin" [PARTLY SUPERSEDED B3: futuristic room, the window shows the ship; see FINAL_STORYLINE SC02–SC03]
**Recommendation:** not a whole house, but **one room at night**: a bedroom-study that has clearly been a developer's workspace for years. A full house adds modelling cost and walking time without adding meaning.

- A desk by a window. Two monitors with real code from your own repositories, plus a terminal.
- Books whose spines carry real titles from your education `[TO CONFIRM]`.
- A corkboard or shelf with personal objects that hint at your story (certificates, sketches, a model rocket, a photo frame). Each is either real or clearly decorative. None implies a false fact.
- **The window** shows the night sky with the Moon visible. This foreshadows the first destination, which is the payoff of the opening shot.
- Warm practical lighting (desk lamp, monitor glow) against cool moonlight through the window. This warm-versus-cold contrast is the visual thesis of the whole film: **home is warm; the universe is cold and vast.**
- The astronaut's suit and helmet are in the room from the first frame.

Identity placement: name and one-line introduction (title card), background and origin (desk), "why I build" (walk to the door). Education is **teased** through the books and **revealed** at the Moon.

### B. The spaceship: "Ignition"
- **House-to-ship transition:** the bedroom door opens onto white light, and the camera passes through. The door frame **match-cuts** into the ship's hatch frame. This is cinematic, cheap (no garden, street or boarding ramp to model) and meaningful: *the door you walk out of every day is the airlock.*
- **Cockpit:** compact, functional and lived-in, in the style of Crew Dragon or Orion rather than Star Wars: touchscreens, physical switches and a single wide window. Displays are **diegetic**:
  - *Mission Profile* screen: your role and specialisation `[TO CONFIRM]`.
  - *Flight Plan* screen: the chapter list, which doubles as the real navigation UI.
- **Exterior** is first revealed after engine ignition, in a wide shot of the ship on the pad. The exterior must look good in 3–4 hero shots only, not from every angle.

### C. Departure from Earth
The launch is pure cinema, with almost no text: ignition, vibration, clouds tearing past, sky gradient blue → indigo → black, and Earth's curvature. Then the **signature shot: an orbital sunrise** over Earth's limb. At most one line of text appears (for example, a mission statement).

### D. Solar system exploration (selected destinations only) [SUPERSEDED B3: one continuous flight; see FINAL_STORYLINE SC06–SC09]
**We do not visit all eight planets.** Visiting every planet makes the film repetitive and long, and forces content onto planets that have no meaning. The route:

```
Earth → Moon → (inward) Sun → slingshot outward → Mars → Asteroid Belt → Jupiter system → Saturn → Heliopause
```

| Stop | Content | Why this body |
|---|---|---|
| **Moon** | Education | Humanity's first destination beyond Earth and the training ground for everything after. Education is your first step off the ground. |
| **Sun** | Core engineering identity | The source of energy that powers every other destination. |
| **Mars** | Professional experience (internships) | The Moon was training; Mars is where people go to **work in real conditions**. Experience = real missions. Outposts on the surface = workplaces. |
| **Asteroid Belt** | Other full-stack work and smaller projects | Many small bodies, fast fly-through, each a small build. Breadth without needing a full scene. |
| **Jupiter system** | Flagship projects (as orbital stations) | Jupiter shapes the whole system; its moons are complete worlds of their own. A project in orbit = something you engineered. |
| **Saturn** | Technical stack | The rings are **layers**. Frontend, backend, databases and cloud become ring bands. The clearest skills visualisation in the solar system. |

Mercury, Venus, Uranus and Neptune can appear as distant points or brief passes to keep the system feeling real, without content.

**Alternative route** (present in the decisions log): Sun last, as the climax before leaving. This is more conventional but loses the slingshot, which is the strongest metaphor. Your core identity is the energy that launches you outward to everything else.

### E. The Sun: "Core identity" [SUPERSEDED B3: the spectroscopy idea is kept but moved to the orbital sunrise (SC06); no slingshot]
The ship never approaches closer than a plausible heat-shielded distance (Parker Solar Probe-style: the ship turns its heat shield toward the Sun and the camera stays in its shadow).

**How the Sun communicates identity without a list of technologies:**
> **Spectroscopy.** Astronomers know what the Sun is made of by reading the dark absorption lines in its light. The ship's instrument splits the sunlight into a spectrum, and its **lines are labelled with your 3–5 core engineering principles/strengths** `[TO CONFIRM]`, e.g. "systems thinking", "user-first", "ships end-to-end".

*"You can tell what a star is made of by its light."* It is scientifically real, visually beautiful (a horizontal rainbow band on black) and says "this is what I am made of" without a single logo. Then the ship performs the **slingshot**, and the Sun's light visibly illuminates the planets ahead.

### F. Beyond the solar system: "The Edge" [SUPERSEDED B3: Voyager beat removed; the edge is now the gate to the anime universe (SC10)]
- **Heliopause crossing**, where the Sun's influence ends, shown as a subtle shift in light and particle direction.
- The ship passes **Voyager 1**, the most distant human-made object. Its Golden Record was humanity's message to the future. Here you say what you're **currently learning** and **what you aspire to** `[TO CONFIRM]`.
- The Sun shrinks to one star among thousands. The Milky Way band dominates. This is a quiet beat, with one sentence on screen.

### G. The unknown universe: "The Unbuilt" [SUPERSEDED B3: replaced by the anime-inspired universe; the line-art → colour idea is kept as the crossing transition]
**The risk:** wormholes and "other universes" quickly become generic purple swirls.
**The proposal:** after the wormhole, the visitor arrives in a universe that is **not finished**. Nebulae, stars and planets exist as **wireframes and construction lines** that resolve into fully rendered matter as the visitor scrolls. The metaphor: *the future isn't discovered, it's engineered.* Your future direction (domains you want to work in, problems you want to solve) appears as the structures that become real.

The look is clearly fictional, visually unlike every previous scene, and directly tied to an engineer's identity. It is also technically cheaper than a photoreal alien universe.

### H. The final transmission [SUPERSEDED B3: happens at the homecoming above Earth (SC13), not inside the unknown universe]
In the newly built universe the astronaut stands on a small platform or on the ship's hull, and **plants a transmitter beacon** (human-made, so it follows the metaphor). It powers on and sends the final transmission:

- Name, professional role `[TO CONFIRM]`
- **Download resume** (primary action)
- GitHub · LinkedIn · Email · other contacts `[TO CONFIRM]`
- "Replay mission" and "Return to Earth (top)"

This screen is real HTML: selectable text, keyboard-accessible links and copyable email. It stays visible however the 3D behaves.

**Recommendation:** merge G and H into one act. The transmission happens *in* the unbuilt universe, so the ending lands quickly and doesn't feel like two endings.

## 7. The astronaut

**Recommendation: suited, visor down, faceless, from the very first frame.**

- **Why faceless:** realistic human faces are the fastest way into the uncanny valley on the web. A reflective gold visor removes facial modelling and animation entirely, and lets any visitor project themselves onto the character.
- **Why suited from the start:** a costume change needs two character models or a complex dressing animation. Instead, the opening image *is* the hook: **an astronaut sitting at a developer desk at night**. It is strange, memorable and instantly says "this is a story". The helmet sits on the desk at first. The camera cuts away as the astronaut lifts it and cuts back with the helmet on, so no helmet-on animation is needed.
- **Personal touch:** a mission patch on the shoulder with your initials or personal emblem `[TO CONFIRM]`, plus a name tape. This is cheap texture work with a strong identity payoff.
- **Suit style:** modern, fitted and pressure-suit-like (in the style of SpaceX or Boeing Starliner suits), not a bulky EVA suit. It reads as "engineer-astronaut", animates better and walks more believably.
- **Visibility rule:** the astronaut appears only when they **act** (house, cockpit, slingshot interior beat, final transmission). In space the **ship is the character**, which saves animation cost and keeps shots wide and epic.

## 8. The spaceship

- **Design language:** a believable near-future crew vehicle that is compact, asymmetric in detail and has visible panel seams, RCS thrusters and heat tiles on one face (which pays off at the Sun). No fins or fighter-jet styling.
- **Required views only:** launch pad wide shot, ascent tracking shot, orbital profile, Sun heat-shield shot, fly-bys and the final hull standing shot.
- **Cockpit:** two seats (one empty, which gives subtle scale and loneliness), a wide window and four or five screens. It doesn't need a full interior, only what the camera sees.
- **Movement:** smooth spline paths with eased acceleration. Occasional RCS puffs when it changes heading add small details that sell physics.

## 10. Project integration (tiered)

Not every project deserves its own world. Three tiers:

| Tier | Treatment | Use for |
|---|---|---|
| **1 – Diorama** | Ship docks at an orbital station; a small interactive 3D diorama of the project's core idea opens in a "Project Bay" overlay | **One** flagship project (at most two) that has a genuinely visual concept |
| **2 – Cinematic dossier** | A station fly-by opens a well-designed panel: real screenshots or a short screen recording, problem, your role, stack, architecture sketch and links | Other major projects |
| **3 – Log entry** | Flagged asteroid → a single line with stack and link; a full list in Mission Brief | Smaller and other full-stack work |

**Preliminary assessment (pending your real project facts):**
- **Smart Way Traffic** is the strongest Tier 1 candidate: traffic flow is inherently visual, and a small procedural intersection or road network diorama can be built largely in code. *It must reflect what the project actually does. I need its real scope.*
- **MeraSports / Sports Paramount** and **Arcslot**: Tier 1 or Tier 2 depending on their real scope, visual potential and the material you have (screenshots, demos).
- **Other full-stack work**: Tier 3.

**Project Bay interaction rules:** opening a project pauses scroll progression. Closing (button, Esc, or scrolling) returns the visitor to the exact frame they left. A project never traps the visitor.

## 12. Scroll behaviour

- Scroll position maps to a **single master timeline**. Every animation (character, camera, ship, lighting, text) is a function of that progress value, so **reversing is automatic and exact**.
- **Scroll budget is uneven by design:** travel segments are fast (little scroll), and information beats are slower (more scroll distance and soft "dwell" zones so text can be read).
- **Text appears at rest points**, not during fast motion. The camera settles, text arrives, the visitor reads, and the next movement begins.
- **Smoothing:** scroll input is smoothed (inertia) so trackpads and mouse wheels both feel cinematic, without the "scroll-jacking" feeling of fixed snapping.
- **No hard scroll locks.** At most there are gentle snap points at chapter rest positions, with an option to disable them.

---

## 13. Navigation & interaction

- **Persistent minimal UI:** top-left the mission name (your name); top-right **Resume** · **Mission Brief** · **Menu**; bottom a thin progress line with the current destination label (e.g. `03 · MOON · EDUCATION`).
- **Chapter menu = Flight Plan:** the same list shown on the cockpit screen. Selecting a chapter jumps there with a fast, brief transition, not a replay of the whole flight.
- **Skip intro:** visible during Act I; jumps to the cockpit.
- **Keyboard:** arrow keys / space advance between rest points; Esc closes overlays; all controls are tabbable.
- **Reduced motion:** honours the OS setting; offers a choice between a static "photo-story" version (key frames plus crossfades) and Mission Brief.
- **Mobile:** a lighter scene set, vertical framing per shot (the camera is re-composed for portrait, not just squeezed), tap targets ≥ 44px, and Mission Brief offered upfront on low-capability devices.
- **Sound (optional, off by default):** a single ambient score and a few diegetic sounds (switches, ignition rumble). It is a strong cinematic multiplier but strictly opt-in.

---

## 14. Keeping it engaging, not long

- **Target runtime:** 3–4 minutes of scrolling for the whole journey at a comfortable pace.
- **Rule of one:** each scene delivers one idea. If a scene needs two ideas, it becomes two scenes or one is moved.
- **Travel is compressed:** cuts and fast transitions between destinations, never long empty flights.
- **Reading load per stop:** a headline plus at most 2–4 short lines on screen. Depth lives in overlays and Mission Brief.
- **Every 20–30 seconds something visually new** (new light, new palette, new scale).

## 15. Creative recommendations (summary)

1. Adopt the **natural = identity / built = work** rule as the backbone.
2. Make the opening image **an astronaut at a developer desk at night**, with the Moon in the window.
3. Use the **door → hatch match cut** instead of modelling a house exterior and boarding sequence.
4. Visit **six destinations**, not every planet. Use the **Sun slingshot** as the midpoint.
5. Show the Sun's identity through **spectroscopy**, not logos.
6. Use **Saturn's rings** for the tech stack and **Mars outposts** for experience.
7. Give projects **tiered treatment**: one diorama, the rest dossiers or log entries.
8. Make the **Voyager 1** pass the "continuous learning" beat.
9. Show the unknown universe as **the Unbuilt**: wireframes resolving into matter.
10. Ship a first-class **Mission Brief** mode from day one.
