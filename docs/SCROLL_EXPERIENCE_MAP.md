> Planning estimates, not implementation values. **Total length: about 34 screens on desktop** (1 screen = one viewport height of scrolling) and about 30 on portrait mobile. A comfortable pace is roughly 3–4.5 minutes. Rest points are where text is readable; all other movement is brief.
> Navigation, Resume, Contact and Mission Brief are reachable at **every** percentage. No section locks the scroll.

## Pacing rules
- **No travel segment longer than about 2 screens without something new** (a new light, body or content).
- **Rest points:** 13 in total. Each holds about 0.6–1 screen of dwell (scroll continues, motion slows).
- **Reading load:** a headline plus at most 2–4 short lines per rest point; details are in panels.
- **Recruiter check:** experience is visible by about 12%, flagship projects by about 36%, and contact at **any** moment through the persistent Contact button.

Jumps cut directly (with a short fade) and never play the frames in between. The **Contact** button in the persistent UI opens a **contact sheet overlay instantly**, without travelling. The SC13 scene is the cinematic version of the same information.

## Reverse scrolling
Every row is reversible: the astronaut walks back, the ship flies backwards along its path, style blends un-blend and text fades back in. There are no one-way triggers.

## Mobile differences
- Portrait framing variants for every shot.
- The belt beacons become a single "Supporting projects" button rather than five world labels.
- The Project Bay uses a full-screen sheet.
- Scroll lengths are about 10% shorter.
